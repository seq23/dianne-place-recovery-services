How Do I Find Transitional Housing for Recovery?

Transitional housing is a supportive living environment for individuals in early recovery from substance use. To get transitional housing, you typically connect with a recovery residence that offers structured, substance-free sober living. You will discuss your needs and see if the program is a good fit for your recovery journey.

Published 1 November 2026 · Dianne's Place Recovery Services

What is Transitional Housing for Recovery?

Transitional housing, often called sober living, provides a safe and structured home for adults committed to early recovery. It is a substance-free environment where you live with others who are also working on their recovery. This type of housing helps you build a stable foundation after leaving a treatment program or detox. It offers a supportive community as you learn to navigate daily life without substances.

The goal is to help you develop healthy routines and coping skills in a real-world setting. Sober living is not a medical facility. It is not detox, treatment, therapy, or emergency care. If you are in immediate danger, please call 911. For crisis support, you can call or text 988. Sober living focuses on peer support and personal responsibility within a structured setting.

Who Can Benefit from Sober Living?

Adults in early recovery from substance use often find sober living helpful. This includes individuals who have completed a treatment program and are looking for a supportive next step. It also includes those who are seeking a structured, substance-free environment to begin or continue their recovery journey. Families and treatment providers often refer individuals to sober living homes.

Sober living is designed for people who are ready to commit to a substance-free lifestyle. It is for those who want to build a strong foundation for long-term recovery. It offers a bridge between more intensive treatment and fully independent living. This setting can help you practice new skills and build a support network in a safe space.

What Does Structured Sober Living Offer?

Structured sober living provides a framework for daily life that supports recovery. This often includes house rules, regular meetings, and expectations for participation in the community. You will live in a substance-free environment with others who share similar goals. This peer support can be a powerful part of the recovery process. You learn from each other and hold each other accountable.

How Do I Take the Next Steps?

Finding the right transitional housing involves understanding your needs and the services offered. Start by researching recovery residences in your area. Look for programs that align with your recovery goals and values. It is important to find a place where you feel comfortable and supported. You can also ask your current treatment team or therapist for recommendations.

When you are ready, reach out to the recovery residence directly. You will likely have a conversation about your recovery journey and what you are looking for in a sober living environment. Frequently asked questions

Is transitional housing the same as detox?

No, transitional housing is not detox. Detox is the first step in safely managing withdrawal symptoms from substances. Transitional housing, or sober living, is a supportive living environment for individuals who have already completed detox and are committed to maintaining a substance-free lifestyle in early recovery.

What kind of support is offered in sober living?

Sober living offers a structured, substance-free environment with peer support. Residents live together and often participate in house meetings, follow house rules, and engage in recovery-focused activities. The support comes from living with others who share a commitment to recovery and from the structured environment itself.

Can my family visit me in transitional housing?

Family visitation policies vary among transitional housing programs. Many sober living homes encourage healthy family involvement as part of the recovery process, often with specific guidelines to maintain the safety and structure of the environment. It is best to discuss visitation policies directly with the specific sober living residence.

How long can I stay in transitional housing?

The length of stay in transitional housing, or sober living, is flexible and depends on your individual recovery needs and the program's structure. Many individuals stay for several months to a year or more, allowing ample time to build a strong foundation for long-term recovery before transitioning to fully independent living.
