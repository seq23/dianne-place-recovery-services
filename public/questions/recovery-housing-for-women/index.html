Recovery Housing for Women in Denver

Recovery housing for women is a structured, substance-free living environment designed to support individuals navigating early recovery. It provides a stable home, peer support, and daily accountability while you build a foundation for long-term sobriety and independence in the community.

Published 3 November 2026 · Dianne's Place Recovery Services

What is women-focused recovery housing?

Recovery housing offers a safe and substance-free place to live while you practice the tools learned in primary treatment. For women in early recovery, living alongside peers with similar goals provides a unique sense of community and mutual understanding. This environment helps reduce isolation and builds confidence during a vulnerable transition time.

A structured home focuses on daily routines, shared responsibilities, and personal accountability. You participate in house meetings, maintain your own space, and follow clear guidelines designed to keep the environment secure for everyone. This steady rhythm helps you adjust to life outside of a controlled facility without the pressure of immediate independence.

Sober living is not a substitute for medical detox, clinical treatment, or therapy. Instead, it serves as a bridge between intensive care and fully independent living. You manage your own daily schedule, attend support meetings in the community, and work toward personal goals while living in a supportive, drug-free residence.

Choosing a women-only space can offer specific emotional safety and focus. Many women find that sharing a home with others who understand specific life experiences makes it easier to share challenges and celebrate milestones. The emphasis remains on mutual respect, personal responsibility, and steady progress.

How does structured sober living work?

Structured sober living relies on clear expectations that protect the well-being of every resident. Homes typically require complete abstinence from alcohol and unprescribed drugs, verified through routine screening. These guidelines create a predictable and secure atmosphere where everyone can focus on their personal growth.

Residents share common areas like kitchens and living rooms, which encourages cooperation and communication. You learn to navigate minor conflicts respectfully and contribute to the upkeep of the home. These everyday tasks help rebuild life skills that may have been neglected during active substance use.

While living in the house, you maintain active involvement in your own recovery plan. This often includes attending mutual-aid meetings, working with a sponsor, or participating in outpatient programs. The structure of the home supports these activities by ensuring quiet hours, curfews, and regular household check-ins.

Independence grows gradually in this setting. You have the freedom to seek employment, attend school, or reconnect with family while returning to a secure, substance-free base each evening. This balance of responsibility and freedom prepares you for lasting stability.

Who is recovery housing for?

Recovery housing is intended for adults in early recovery who no longer require medical detox or intensive inpatient treatment. If you have completed a primary program and need a safe place to practice daily sobriety, transitional housing provides the necessary buffer against early triggers.

Families often look into sober living when a loved one is preparing to leave a clinical facility. Knowing that there is a structured environment with clear guidelines offers peace of mind. Families can focus on rebuilding trust while their relative lives in a supportive, accountable community.

Treatment and discharge staff frequently recommend recovery housing as part of a comprehensive continuing care plan. Referrers look for homes that maintain high standards of safety, peer support, and clear policies. This helps ensure a smooth transition from a controlled clinical setting to community-based living.

No matter your background, if you are committed to maintaining a substance-free lifestyle and willing to follow household guidelines, recovery housing can be a valuable step. The focus is on daily action, personal dignity, and moving forward at your own pace.

Frequently asked questions

Is recovery housing the same as inpatient treatment?

No. Recovery housing provides structured, substance-free sober living, but it does not offer medical detox, clinical therapy, or medical treatment. It is a supportive residential environment for people who have already completed primary care and want to practice daily sobriety.

What rules do residents follow in sober living?

Residents agree to maintain complete abstinence from alcohol and unprescribed drugs, participate in household meetings, share chores, and follow curfews. These clear expectations help maintain a safe, accountable, and supportive environment for everyone in the home.

Can family members help with the transition?

Yes. Treatment providers and family members often assist with referral support and planning. Families can help explore options, review application steps, and coordinate with staff to ensure a smooth move into the transitional living environment.

What should I do in an emergency?

Recovery housing is not equipped for emergencies. If you are facing immediate danger, call 911. For crisis support and guidance related to mental health or substance use, call or text 988 to reach the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.
